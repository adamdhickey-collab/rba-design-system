The photography style

The library is built as one photographic world in named families — the filter row above the grid is the current list — not a pile of images that happen to look alike. Consistency comes from lighting, composition, authenticity and subject matter, not from forcing every frame through the same color grade: a slightly imperfect photo of two people genuinely working beats an exquisitely lit photo of five models pretending to meet.

RuleWhat it means when choosing an image
Candid over posedPeople engaged with the work or each other, never the camera. Reject synchronized smiles, handshakes, and pointing at meaningless charts.
Human-centeredThe person always outranks the machinery, the screen and the office. An engineer with a tablet beats a dramatic robot arm with nobody around.
Environmental portraitsShow people in their working context with the tools of their craft — not studio headshots, not arms-folded executives.
Technology in human usePhotograph people using software and devices. No robot heads, glowing brains, blue circuitry faces, holograms, floating binary or neon cyber-tunnels — they undermine RBA’s pragmatic AI positioning.
Contemporary & realNatural light, modern workplaces, believable clutter. If it feels obviously like stock, it fails, however beautiful it is.
No forced gradingUse images as supplied. Trying to make everything beige, blue or desaturated makes a library feel more artificial, not less.
Abstract suggests, never illustratesAbstract frames use architecture, glass, reflections, light, patterns and organic structures to suggest technology, connectivity and momentum — sophisticated, warm, and grounded in the real world. No neon circuitry, digital globes, binary rain or cyberpunk. They earn their place as visual breathing room: section headers, thought leadership, AI and data topics.

Before licensing

CheckWhat to confirm
AI disclosureThe review found no explicit disclosure on any asset page, which is not the same as confirming there is none. Check each one on Adobe Stock before buying — the selection brief excludes AI-generated imagery.
Licence typeEvery image here is an unlicensed comp. Several are Stocksy, Westend61 or peopleimages frames — premium collections that Adobe resells but a standard credit does not cover. Confirm the tier before assuming one does.
Model releasesAny image containing recognisable people needs one. Confirm the release covers the intended use, including any paid or out-of-home placement.
ResolutionHover a card for its dimensions. Check the crop you actually intend still leaves enough pixels; don’t buy on the raw number alone.
DuplicationStandard-collection images are licensed to anyone. For anything carrying the brand — homepage, campaign hero — prefer the premium picks, which are far less widely used.

Using an image

RuleRequirement
CroppingCrop freely, but keep the subject. Don’t crop so tight that a photograph of people becomes a photograph of a shoulder. Hover a card for the reviewer’s crop note.
ColorUse them as supplied. No filters, no duotone, no recoloring to force a photo toward the palette.
Type over imageOnly over a quiet, dark area, in the reversed logo’s white. If it needs a heavy scrim to read, pick a different image.
ResolutionDon’t scale a small image up — go back to the source and take a larger render.
RightsOnly licensed images may ship. A card on this page is not a licence, and neither is a watermarked comp.
