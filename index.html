Colors

The palette as published, with the two usage rules that come attached to it. Click any swatch to copy its hex.

rule

Two rules that aren't preferences. RBA Red belongs to the logo — never a button, heading, link, chart series or border. And Aqua is decorative only: it fails contrast as text, so when a teal has to carry type, use Accent #0D8FA9 instead.

Primary

Midnight is the anchor and carries most of the surface area. The blues below it do the interactive work.

Neutrals

Text, rules and surfaces. Black is #333333 — pure black isn't in the palette.

Gradients

Runs vertically, top to bottom. The angle is part of the specification — a diagonal version is a different asset, not a variation.

Sky · #E6F8FB → #9DDBF6 · 180°

The canonical set also defines --grad-hero (#E6F8FB → #BDE4F1), which is the same ramp stopping short. It is still in the token table, but it isn’t shown here: side by side the two invite a choice that doesn’t matter, and its end stop is already on this page as the Sky Blue swatch. Reach for Sky.

Data visualization

An ordered set, AA-compliant against the page, each value paired with a fill pattern. Use them in order rather than picking by eye.

warning

The patterns are not optional, and not just for print. Measured as a categorical set, these eight do not separate on colour alone: the closest pair, --viz-5 and --viz-8, sit at a perceptual distance of 2.2 where about 15 is the readable floor, and no reordering gets the worst neighbouring pair above 7.9. That is a property of eight values drawn from one narrow blue-slate range, not of the ordering. So a series is identified by pattern plus a direct label, and colour is the thing that makes the chart look like RBA rather than the thing that tells the series apart. Past about four series, split the chart rather than reaching for --viz-5.

In use

Three worked examples. Every series carries a pattern and a label, the grid stays out of the way, and values sit in text ink rather than the series colour. Hover or tab any mark for its value; every figure can also be read as a table.

Typography

Two typefaces. Montserrat carries everything; Libre Caslon Text appears in exactly one place. Both are Google Fonts, so there's nothing to license.

Montserrat

Sans · 100–900, all with italics

Headings, body copy, interface, labels, decks, documents. Everything unless the rule below applies.

download Download Montserrat View on Google Fonts

Libre Caslon Text

Serif · 400 and 400 italic. Regular only — never bold.

The hero display, and nothing else. Not sub-heads, not pull quotes, not body copy, not UI. One place.

download Download Libre Caslon Text View on Google Fonts

Both faces are open-licensed under the SIL Open Font License, so they can be installed on any machine and embedded in any deck or document without a purchase. The download gives you the full family as .ttf; on the specimen page, Get font → Download all is the same file.

Rules

RuleRequirement
The serifHero display only. If you're reaching for Libre Caslon anywhere else, the answer is Montserrat.
Serif weightRegular, always. Never bold the serif — the family has no ExtraBold, so software fakes one by smearing the outline, and a faked bold is a different letterform.
Heading weight800, not 700 — this is a Montserrat rule. The scale is built on ExtraBold; Bold reads as a different brand.
Heading colorMidnight. Not black, and never red.
MeasureBody copy caps around 68 characters. Wider needs a second column, not a longer line.
SubstitutionBoth faces are free and open-licensed — install them rather than accepting Calibri or Georgia.

Logos

The mark, imported from the brand's own artwork. One colorway per background it has to survive — hover any card to download that colorway as an SVG.

The mark is one color

Wordmark and device are a single fill, always. There is no two-color version, so the only decision is which color — and that follows the background, not preference. RBA Red is reserved for the mark itself: it is the one place in the system that red is allowed to appear.

A separate asset, not a colorway — the mark knocked out of a red field. Use it where the mark needs to hold its own square: an avatar, an app icon, a favicon, a stamp in the corner of a photograph.

Rules

RuleRequirement
ColorOne fill, never two. Red on light backgrounds, white on midnight, black, and photography. No other color, and never a gradient.
Red is the mark'sRBA Red exists for the logo. Don't borrow it for a button, a heading, a link, or a chart — the palette has blues for all of that.
Clear spaceThe height of the "R" on all four sides. Nothing enters it — not type, not an image edge, not another logo.
Minimum size120px wide on screen, 1in in print. Below that the device stops resolving and reads as a smudge.
Over photographyReversed, on a part of the image that is dark and quiet. If the mark needs a scrim to be legible, the photo is wrong for it.
Which lockupThe horizontal mark by default. The block lockup only where a square is required.
NeverRedraw or retype it. Recolor individual paths. Stretch, skew, rotate, outline, or add a shadow or glow. Rebuild it from a screenshot. Place it on a background that breaks its contrast.
